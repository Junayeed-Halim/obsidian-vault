This is a practice-based studio subject focused on practical software development. You will learn primarily by doing, through collaborative work in small groups. You are expected to develop a deep understanding of software development and become professionally competent. To support these goals, the studio emphasizes reflective design practice, teamwork, mentoring, and deep learning techniques, including immersion in challenging problems within a complex environment.

## Delivery

Software Development Studio (SDS) is a practiced-oriented subject. We have:

- The only lecture with the entire cohort which is held in Week 1, where the subject coordinator covers the key aspects of the subject and welcomes several clients to present their projects.
- Weekly 3-hour studio sessions from Week 2 to Week 13, where you work in teams to develop software under the guidance of industry- and academic- experienced tutors.

SDS provides a learning environment similar to a workplace. You will work in development roles appropriate to your stage of learning and experience and may participate in multi-year teams, progressing in responsibility as you demonstrate professional competencies and graduate attributes. You may use the Software Development Studio room (CB11.05.402) outside class time to collaborate with your team, write regular reflections on your SDS experience, and participate in peer evaluations of teamwork.

## Preparation and participation

SDS does not involve traditional lecturing or tutoring. Instead, professional agile software development materials, tools, coaching, and mentoring are provided to support you in delivering your projects successfully. To make the most of this environment, you are expected to:

- 

**Take initiative:** If you are a self-sufficient team, that's great--just go for it! If you need help, we will provide some pointers on potential options to explore, but you will evaluate these options and make the decisions. Clients may be technical or non-technical; in any case, be proactive and responsive.

- 

**Handle scoping:** Know what to prioritize and estimate what you can realistically achieve.

- 

**Report issues early:** Ask for technical guidance and report complex situations related to your team and your clients early.

## Stay on track

If circumstances prevent you from maintaining attendance and/or keeping up with assessment, you need to inform and/or meet with your tutor or subject coordinator to ensure you can stay on track to pass the subject. Further assistance with study support can be found at [Course and subject advice](https://www.uts.edu.au/current-students/support/academic-support/course-and-subject-advice).

## Feedback

Continuous weekly feedback and mentoring is provided to improve your work before the final presentation and deliverable handover.  Assessment feedback on submissions shall be provided with marks by the mentor/marker within 2 weeks after the assessment item due or submission date.
