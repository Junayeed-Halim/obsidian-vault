# Hoeffding’s Inequality: A Formal Bound on Empirical Risk

This is an optional technical reading section. We show the derivation of Hoeffding’s inequality and its application to bounding the deviation of empirical risk for the binary classification problem. We recap the main result that we are after: for any i.i.d. sequence \(Z_1, \dots, Z_N\) taking values in a bounded interval \([a, b]\),

\begin{align} P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i - \mathbb{E}[Z]\right| > \epsilon\right) \;\le\; 2\exp\!\left(\frac{-2N\epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2}\right). \end{align}

We build up to the main result in three steps: (1) the Chernoff bounding trick, which turns a probability statement into an expectation; (2) Hoeffding’s lemma, which bounds the moment-generating function of a bounded random variable; and (3) the final assembly of Hoeffding’s inequality.

## Step 1: The Chernoff Bounding Trick

Let \(X\) be a real-valued random variable. We want to bound the probability that \(X\) is large, specifically \(P(X \ge a)\) for some threshold \(a\).

The Chernoff trick is to convert this probability into an expectation. Note that for any \(t > 0\), the events \(\{X \ge a\}\) and \(\{e^{tX} \ge e^{ta}\}\) are identical. Therefore, by Markov’s inequality (which states that for any non-negative random variable \(Z\), \(P(Z \ge c) \le \mathbb{E}[Z]/c\)):

\begin{align} P(X \ge a) = P(e^{tX} \ge e^{ta}) \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]}{e^{ta}} = e^{-ta}\, \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]. \label{eq:chernoff} \end{align}

This holds for every \(t > 0\). The function \(\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\) is the moment-generating function (MGF) of \(X\), evaluated at \(t\).

Why is this useful? Because the MGF is often much easier to bound analytically than the tail probability directly. Furthermore, \eqref{eq:chernoff} holds for all \(t > 0\), so we are free to choose \(t\) to minimise the right-hand side and obtain the tightest possible bound.

Applying it to a mean. In the context of empirical risk, we want to bound \(P(E_{in} - E_{out} \ge \epsilon)\). Let \(Z_i = L(h(x_i), y_i) - E_{out}[h]\) be the centred loss at sample \(i\) (so that \(\mathbb{E}[Z_i] = 0\) by unbiasedness). Then \(E_{in} - E_{out} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i\), and applying \eqref{eq:chernoff} to \(X = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i Z_i\) with threshold \(a = \epsilon\):

\begin{align} P(E_{in} - E_{out} \ge \epsilon) \le e^{-tN\epsilon}\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{t\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i}\right] = e^{-tN\epsilon}\, \prod_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}[e^{tZ_i}], \label{eq:chernoff_product} \end{align}

where the last equality uses the independence of \(Z_1, \ldots, Z_N\) (since the training examples are i.i.d.). We have reduced the problem to bounding the MGF of a single centred bounded random variable \(Z_i\).

## Step 2: Hoeffding’s Lemma

Lemma (Hoeffding’s Lemma).

Let \(Z\) be a random variable with \(a \le Z \le b\) and \(\mathbb{E}[Z] = 0\). Then for any \(t > 0\),

\begin{align} \mathbb{E}[e^{tZ}] \le \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2(b-a)^2}{8}\right). \label{eq:hoeffding_lemma} \end{align}

Proof sketch. The proof has three components.

**Component 1: Use convexity to bound \(e^{tZ}\) pointwise.** The exponential function \(x \mapsto e^{tx}\) is convex. For any \(z \in [a,b]\), convexity implies that the graph of \(e^{tx}\) lies below the chord connecting \((a, e^{ta})\) and \((b, e^{tb})\):

\begin{align} e^{tz} \le \frac{b-z}{b-a}\, e^{ta} + \frac{z-a}{b-a}\, e^{tb}. \label{eq:convex_bound} \end{align}

**Component 2: Take expectations and use \(\mathbb{E}[Z] = 0\).** Taking expectations of both sides of \eqref{eq:convex_bound} and using \(\mathbb{E}[Z] = 0\):

\begin{align} \mathbb{E}[e^{tZ}] \le \frac{b - \mathbb{E}[Z]}{b-a}\, e^{ta} + \frac{\mathbb{E}[Z]-a}{b-a}\, e^{tb} = \frac{b}{b-a}\, e^{ta} - \frac{a}{b-a}\, e^{tb}. \label{eq:mgf_bound} \end{align}

**Component 3: Bound the right-hand side by \(e^{t^2(b-a)^2/8}\).** Write the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:mgf_bound} as \(e^{\phi(t)}\) where

\[ \phi(t) = ta + \log\!\left(\frac{b}{b-a} - \frac{a}{b-a}\,e^{t(b-a)}\right). \]

One can verify that \(\phi(0) = 0\), \(\phi'(0) = 0\) (using \(\frac{b}{b-a} - \frac{a}{b-a} = 1\) and the definition of \(\phi'\)), and the second derivative satisfies \(\phi''(t) \le \frac{(b-a)^2}{4}\) everywhere (the maximum is achieved by the concavity of \(v(1-v)\) for \(v \in [0,1]\), which is maximised at \(v = 1/2\) giving \(1/4\)). A Taylor expansion then gives:

\[ \phi(t) = \phi(0) + \phi'(0)\,t + \frac{\phi''(\xi)}{2}\,t^2 \le \frac{t^2(b-a)^2}{8} \]

for some \(\xi \in (0,t)\). Therefore \(\mathbb{E}[e^{tZ}] \le e^{\phi(t)} \le e^{t^2(b-a)^2/8}\), which is \eqref{eq:hoeffding_lemma}.

\(\square\)

## Step 3: Hoeffding’s Inequality

We now assemble the final result.

Theorem (Hoeffding’s Inequality).

Let \(Z_1, Z_2, \ldots, Z_N\) be independent random variables with \(a_i \le Z_i \le b_i\) and \(\mathbb{E}[Z_i] = \mu_i\). Let \(\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mu_i\). Then for any \(\epsilon > 0\),

\begin{align} P\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i - \mu \ge \epsilon\right) \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{2N^2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (b_i - a_i)^2}\right). \label{eq:hoeffding_general} \end{align}

Applying the same argument to \(-Z_i\) and taking a union bound gives the two-sided version:

\begin{align} P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i - \mu\right| \ge \epsilon\right) \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2N^2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (b_i - a_i)^2}\right). \label{eq:hoeffding_twosided} \end{align}

Proof. Applying the Chernoff bound from \eqref{eq:chernoff_product} to centred variables \(Z_i - \mu_i\) and using Hoeffding’s lemma \eqref{eq:hoeffding_lemma}:

\begin{align} P\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N (Z_i - \mu_i) \ge \epsilon\right) &\le e^{-tN\epsilon} \prod_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}[e^{t(Z_i-\mu_i)}] \nonumber \\ &\le e^{-tN\epsilon} \prod_{i=1}^N e^{t^2(b_i-a_i)^2/8} \nonumber \\ &= \exp\!\left(-tN\epsilon + \frac{t^2}{8}\sum_{i=1}^N(b_i-a_i)^2\right). \end{align}

This holds for all \(t > 0\). To get the tightest bound, minimise over \(t\) by taking the derivative with respect to \(t\) and setting it to zero: \(t^* = \frac{4N\epsilon}{\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2}.\) Substituting back gives \eqref{eq:hoeffding_general}.

\(\square\)

## Application to Empirical Risk of Binary Classification

As a direct application, consider binary classification with the 0-1 loss, where \(L(h(x), y) \in \{0, 1\}\). Then \(Z_i = L(h(x_i), y_i) \in [0,1]\), so \(a_i = 0\), \(b_i = 1\) and \((b_i - a_i)^2 = 1\) for all \(i\). The two-sided Hoeffding inequality \eqref{eq:hoeffding_twosided} becomes:

\begin{align} P\!\left(|E_{in}[h;D] - E_{out}[h]| \ge \epsilon\right) \le 2e^{-2N\epsilon^2}. \label{eq:hoeffding_binary} \end{align}

This is a remarkably clean and informative bound. Reading it carefully:

- The probability decreases *exponentially* in \(N\epsilon^2\): doubling the training set size is equivalent to increasing \(\epsilon\) by a factor of \(\sqrt{2}\) at the same confidence level.
- The bound is *independent of the dimension \(d\)* of the input \(x_i\). Whether the input is a scalar or a million-dimensional image, the same \(N\) gives the same probabilistic guarantee. This confirms the conclusion of the previous section: the curse of dimensionality does not apply to the accuracy of empirical risk as an estimator.
- The bound holds for a *fixed* hypothesis \(h\). To handle the full ERM problem, where \(\hat{h}\) is chosen by minimising \(E_{in}\) over \(\mathcal{H}\), a more refined argument (union bound over \(\mathcal{H}\) and the VC theory) is required. This is the subject of PAC learning theory.

Practical reading. Rearranging \eqref{eq:hoeffding_binary}: with probability at least \(1 - \delta\), the deviation of empirical risk from true risk satisfies

\begin{align} |E_{in}[h;D] - E_{out}[h]| \le \sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2N}}. \label{eq:hoeffding_confidence} \end{align}

For \(\delta = 0.05\) (95% confidence) and \(N = 1000\), the right-hand side is approximately \(\sqrt{\frac{3.69}{2000}} \approx 0.043\). That is, with 95% probability, the empirical risk on 1000 training examples differs from the true risk by at most about 4.3 percentage points — for any fixed hypothesis and any data distribution.
